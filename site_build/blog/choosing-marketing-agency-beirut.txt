12:T1aad,<p>To choose a marketing agency in Beirut, judge them on how they measure results, who owns your accounts, and how clearly they explain what they will do in the first 90 days. A good agency asks more questions about your business than you ask about theirs, talks about sales rather than likes, and is comfortable putting its plan and reporting in writing.</p>
<p>Beirut has a lot of agencies, from one person studios to large creative shops. Many do beautiful work. Fewer can show you, with numbers, how their work turned into revenue. The questions below help you tell the difference in one or two meetings.</p>
<p>Print this list, bring it to your calls, and take notes on the answers.</p>
<h2>1. &quot;How will we measure success, and what will you report every month?&quot;</h2>
<p>This is the most important question. A strong answer names business numbers: sales, leads, cost per sale, cost per lead, revenue from ads. It also explains how they will track those numbers, including sales that happen on WhatsApp or by phone.</p>
<p>A weak answer talks mostly about reach, impressions, followers and engagement. Those numbers have their place, but they do not pay salaries.</p>
<p><strong>Good sign:</strong> they ask what your average order value is, what your margins look like, and how many leads your team can handle per week.</p>
<p><strong>Red flag:</strong> they promise a number of followers or &quot;viral&quot; content.</p>
<h2>2. &quot;Who owns the ad accounts, pixel, page and website?&quot;</h2>
<p>You should, every time. Your Meta Business portfolio, ad account, pixel and dataset, Google Ads account, Google Analytics, domain and website hosting should be in your name, with the agency added as a partner or user.</p>
<p>If an agency runs ads from their own ad account, you lose all the history and learning the day you leave. That history is valuable, and starting from zero costs money.</p>
<p><strong>Good sign:</strong> they ask you to create the accounts (or help you create them under your ownership) and request partner access.</p>
<p><strong>Red flag:</strong> &quot;Don&#39;t worry, we&#39;ll run it from our account.&quot;</p>
<h2>3. &quot;Who exactly will work on my account?&quot;</h2>
<p>In many agencies, the senior person sells you and a junior person runs the work. That can be fine if the junior person is supervised. Ask to meet the person who will manage your campaigns day to day, and ask how many other accounts they handle.</p>
<h2>4. &quot;What will you do in the first 30, 60 and 90 days?&quot;</h2>
<p>You want a plan, even a simple one. Something like:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>First 30 days:</strong> audit, fix tracking, set up campaigns, launch first creative tests</li>
<li><strong>Days 30 to 60:</strong> cut what is not working, double down on what is, improve the landing page or WhatsApp process</li>
<li><strong>Days 60 to 90:</strong> scale budget on winners, add new channels or offers</li>
</ul>
<p>An agency that cannot describe its first month is going to make it up as it goes, on your budget.</p>
<h2>5. &quot;How do you handle creative?&quot;</h2>
<p>Ads live or die on creative: the video, images and words people actually see. Ask who makes it, how many new ads you will get per month, and whether you need to provide raw footage. Also ask how they decide what to make next. The best answer is &quot;we look at what is working in your account and in your market, and we test new angles every month.&quot;</p>
<h2>6. &quot;Can you show me results from a business similar to mine?&quot;</h2>
<p>Ask for a case study or a screen share of a real account (with private details hidden). Look for numbers over time, ideally cost per result and revenue, not a single screenshot of a good day.</p>
<p>Be careful with very large claims. A good agency will also tell you about a campaign that did not work and what they learned.</p>
<h2>7. &quot;What happens if it doesn&#39;t work?&quot;</h2>
<p>Ask about the contract length, the notice period and how they handle a slow start. A three month minimum is common and reasonable, since ads need time to learn. A twelve month lock in with no exit is a lot to ask from someone you have not worked with yet.</p>
<h2>8. &quot;What do you need from me?&quot;</h2>
<p>Good agencies are clear about this. They will need fast approvals, access to accounts, product information, maybe some filming time, and feedback on lead quality. If your team handles WhatsApp sales, they will also want to know how quickly you reply, since that affects results as much as the ads do.</p>
<h2>A worked example: comparing two proposals</h2>
<p><em>The proposals below are hypothetical, to show how to compare them.</em></p>
<p>You run a clinic in Achrafieh and get two proposals.</p>
<p><strong>Agency A</strong> charges $800 per month. They promise 20 posts, 30 stories and &quot;a growing audience.&quot; Ads are included &quot;up to $300 spend.&quot; Reporting is a monthly PDF of reach and engagement.</p>
<p><strong>Agency B</strong> charges $1,200 per month plus ad spend paid directly by you. They will set up tracking for booked appointments, build two lead campaigns, produce eight new ad creatives per month, and report weekly on cost per booked appointment. They ask about your average patient value, which is $250.</p>
<p>Agency A looks cheaper. But if Agency B gets you 15 extra booked patients per month at that $250 value, that is $3,750 in new revenue against their fee plus ad spend, and you can see it in a report. Agency A might get you more followers, and you have no idea whether a single one of them became a patient.</p>
<p>The question to ask yourself is which proposal is tied to the number that pays your bills.</p>
<h2>Red flags worth walking away from</h2>
<ul>
<li>They guarantee specific results before seeing your account</li>
<li>They will not give you ownership of your accounts</li>
<li>Reporting is only reach, impressions and likes</li>
<li>They cannot explain the pixel, the Conversions API or how they would track WhatsApp sales</li>
<li>Every package is the same no matter your business</li>
<li>They are hard to reach during the sales process (it rarely improves after you sign)</li>
</ul>
<h2>What good looks like</h2>
<p>The right agency feels like a partner who understands your margins and your customers. They tell you when something is not working before you notice. They explain things in plain words. And they care about what happens after the click, whether that is your website, your <a href="/services/paid-ads">paid ads</a> setup or your team&#39;s follow up on WhatsApp.</p>
