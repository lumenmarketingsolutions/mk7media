12:T1f97,<p>To run Meta ads in Lebanon that actually sell, you need three things in place before you spend real money: tracking that tells Meta who bought, an offer that is clear in the first three seconds of the ad, and a sales process on WhatsApp that answers fast and closes. Most accounts that &quot;don&#39;t work&quot; in Lebanon are missing one of those, and the ads get blamed.</p>
<p>The good news is that Lebanon is a small, very social market. People spend a lot of time on Instagram and Facebook, they message businesses on WhatsApp without thinking twice, and ad costs are usually lower than in the Gulf or the US. When the basics are right, a modest budget can go a long way.</p>
<p>This guide walks through the setup we use, in the order we use it.</p>
<h2>Step 1: Decide what a &quot;result&quot; means for your business</h2>
<p>Before you open Ads Manager, write down the one action that makes you money. For an online store it is a purchase. For a clinic it is a booked appointment. For a furniture showroom it might be a WhatsApp conversation that turns into a visit.</p>
<p>This matters because Meta optimizes for whatever you tell it to. If you choose &quot;engagement,&quot; it finds people who like and comment. If you choose &quot;messages,&quot; it finds people who start chats. If you choose &quot;purchases,&quot; it finds people who buy. The algorithm is very good at getting you exactly what you ask for, so ask for the thing you actually want.</p>
<h2>Step 2: Get your tracking right</h2>
<p>Meta needs to see your sales to learn who your buyers are. That happens through two pieces:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The pixel</strong>, a small piece of code on your website that reports actions like &quot;viewed product,&quot; &quot;added to cart&quot; and &quot;purchased.&quot;</li>
<li><strong>The Conversions API</strong>, which sends the same events directly from your server or store platform to Meta. Browsers and phones block a lot of pixel data now, so the server side connection fills the gaps.</li>
</ul>
<p>Both feed into what Meta calls a <strong>dataset</strong> in Events Manager. Inside Events Manager you will also see an <strong>Event Match Quality</strong> score for each event. It tells you how well Meta can match your events to real people, based on details like email, phone number and location that you send with the event. Higher is better, and adding a hashed phone number usually helps a lot in Lebanon, since almost every customer gives you one.</p>
<p>If you sell through WhatsApp instead of a website, you can still report sales back to Meta. This is a core part of the <a href="/services/whatsapp-automation">WhatsApp automation</a> work we do, and we explain the full method in our guide on <a href="/blog/track-whatsapp-sales-meta-ads">tracking WhatsApp sales back to your Meta ads</a>.</p>
<h2>Step 3: Pick the right campaign type</h2>
<p>For most Lebanese businesses, two campaign types do the heavy lifting:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Sales campaigns optimized for purchases</strong> if you have a website with checkout and working tracking. Meta&#39;s Advantage+ options here let the system handle much of the audience and placement work.</li>
<li><strong>Campaigns that send people to WhatsApp</strong> (often called click to WhatsApp ads) if your customers prefer to ask questions before buying, which is very common for higher priced items, services and anything with sizing or customization.</li>
</ol>
<p>Many businesses run both. The website campaign catches people ready to buy now, and the WhatsApp campaign catches people who want to talk first.</p>
<h2>Step 4: Keep targeting broad and let creative do the work</h2>
<p>A few years ago, the skill in Meta ads was picking interests. Today, broad targeting usually wins. You set the country, maybe a city or radius, an age range if it truly matters, and let Meta find buyers.</p>
<p>What decides who sees your ad now is the ad itself. A video about bridal makeup will be shown to people who respond to bridal content. A carousel of men&#39;s sneakers will find sneaker buyers. This is why people say &quot;creative is targeting.&quot; Your job is to make ads that speak clearly to the buyer you want, and Meta&#39;s job is to find that person.</p>
<p>In Lebanon, a few creative patterns tend to perform well:</p>
<ul>
<li>Real people, real faces, and the owner or team on camera</li>
<li>Showing the product being used in a recognizable local setting</li>
<li>Price shown clearly, in the currency you actually charge</li>
<li>Arabic, English, French, or a natural mix, matching how your customers actually speak</li>
<li>Vertical video made for phones, since nearly all traffic is on mobile</li>
</ul>
<h2>Step 5: Set a budget that gives Meta enough data</h2>
<p>Meta learns from conversions. If your budget is so small that you get one sale a week, the system has very little to learn from.</p>
<p>A useful rule of thumb: try to give each ad set enough budget to get several conversions per week. If that is not possible with purchases, optimize for an earlier step like &quot;add to cart&quot; or &quot;conversation started&quot; until volume grows.</p>
<h2>A worked example</h2>
<p><em>The numbers below are an example to show the math, not results from a real client.</em></p>
<p>Say you sell skincare online in Lebanon. Your average order is $45 and your product cost plus delivery is $18, so you keep $27 per order before ads.</p>
<p>That $27 is the most you can spend to get one sale and still break even. If you want to make $10 profit per order, your target cost per purchase is $17.</p>
<p>Now you run a sales campaign at $30 per day for 14 days, which is $420 total. You get 28 purchases. Your cost per purchase is $15, under your $17 target, so this campaign is profitable and worth scaling slowly, perhaps by 20 percent every few days.</p>
<p>If instead you got 12 purchases, your cost per purchase would be $35. That is above break even. Before killing the campaign, you would check: is tracking reporting every sale? Are people adding to cart but not buying (a website or price problem)? Or are they not clicking at all (a creative problem)?</p>
<h2>Step 6: Fix the sales process behind the ads</h2>
<p>This is the step most people skip. In Lebanon and the Gulf, a large share of buyers message before they buy. If they message at 9pm and get a reply the next afternoon, many of them have already bought somewhere else.</p>
<p>Look at:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Response time.</strong> Aim for minutes, not hours, during the hours your ads run.</li>
<li><strong>Saved replies</strong> for prices, delivery areas and delivery times, so answers are fast and consistent.</li>
<li><strong>Cash on delivery.</strong> It is very common locally, and offering it can raise orders. It also brings returns and refused deliveries, so track those.</li>
<li><strong>Follow up.</strong> Someone who asked about price and went quiet is still a warm lead. A polite message the next day often closes the sale.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Step 7: Read the right numbers each week</h2>
<p>You do not need twenty metrics. Watch these:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cost per result</strong> (purchase, lead or conversation) compared to your target</li>
<li><strong>Click through rate</strong>, which tells you whether the ad is grabbing attention</li>
<li><strong>Conversion rate</strong> on your site or in WhatsApp, which tells you whether the offer and the sales process are working</li>
<li><strong>Frequency</strong>, meaning how many times the same person saw your ad. When it climbs and results drop, it is time for new creative</li>
</ul>
<p>Change one thing at a time, give it a few days, and keep notes. That habit alone puts you ahead of most advertisers. If you want a second pair of eyes on your setup, our <a href="/services/paid-ads">paid ads team</a> does this every day.</p>
