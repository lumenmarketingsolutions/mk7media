12:T1f1d,<p>Meta ads (the ads you run on Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and WhatsApp) are organized in three layers. The <strong>campaign</strong> sets your goal, the <strong>ad set</strong> decides who sees the ads, where and with what budget, and the <strong>ad</strong> is the actual video or image people see. The <strong>pixel</strong> and <strong>Conversions API</strong> report what people do after they click, using <strong>events</strong> like &quot;purchase&quot; or &quot;lead,&quot; so Meta can find more people like your buyers.</p>
<p>That is the whole system in one paragraph. Everything else is detail.</p>
<p>If you run a business and want to understand what your agency or freelancer is doing, or you want to try running ads yourself, this guide explains each piece in plain words.</p>
<h2>The three layers: campaign, ad set, ad</h2>
<p>Think of it like a shop window display.</p>
<ul>
<li>The <strong>campaign</strong> is the reason for the display: to sell more, to get people inside, or to get noticed.</li>
<li>The <strong>ad set</strong> is the decision about which street the window faces, how long it stays up and how much you spend on it.</li>
<li>The <strong>ad</strong> is what is actually in the window.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Campaign: your goal</h3>
<p>When you create a campaign, Meta asks you to choose an objective. The main ones are Awareness, Traffic, Engagement, Leads, App promotion and Sales.</p>
<p>This choice matters a lot, because Meta will look for people most likely to take the action you choose. Pick Traffic and you get people who click. Pick Sales and you get people more likely to buy. Most businesses that want revenue should be using Sales or Leads.</p>
<p>You can also set the budget at campaign level, which lets Meta move money between ad sets toward whatever is working best.</p>
<h3>Ad set: who, where, when and how much</h3>
<p>Inside each campaign you have one or more ad sets. Here you decide:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Audience:</strong> location, age, gender and optionally interests. These days broad audiences often work best, because Meta&#39;s system is good at finding buyers on its own when it has good data.</li>
<li><strong>Placements:</strong> where the ads appear, such as Instagram Reels, the Facebook feed or Stories. Letting Meta choose automatically (Advantage+ placements) is usually a good default.</li>
<li><strong>Optimization event:</strong> the specific action you want, such as purchase, add to cart or lead.</li>
<li><strong>Budget and schedule:</strong> daily or lifetime budget, and start and end dates.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Ad: what people actually see</h3>
<p>The ad is the creative (video, image or carousel), the text above it, the headline and the button, like &quot;Shop now&quot; or &quot;Send message.&quot; It also says where people go after they tap: your website, a lead form, Instagram DMs or WhatsApp.</p>
<p>Your creative does more than look nice. It decides who stops scrolling. A video that speaks to new moms will be shown more to new moms, because they are the ones who watch and engage. That is why people say &quot;creative is the new targeting.&quot;</p>
<h2>The learning phase</h2>
<p>When you launch or make a big change to an ad set, Meta goes through a <strong>learning phase</strong>. It is testing who responds, and results can be unstable. Meta generally needs around 50 optimization events in a week for an ad set to exit learning.</p>
<p>In practice this means two things. Avoid editing ads every day, since big edits can restart learning. And if you cannot get many purchases per week, consider optimizing for an earlier step until volume grows.</p>
<h2>The pixel: your website&#39;s reporter</h2>
<p>The <strong>Meta pixel</strong> is a small piece of code on your website. When someone visits, it reports what they do back to Meta.</p>
<p>Without it, Meta can tell you who clicked your ad, but not who bought. With it, Meta can tell you which ads led to sales and use that information to find more buyers.</p>
<h2>Events: the actions you track</h2>
<p>An <strong>event</strong> is one action a person takes. Meta has standard events that most businesses use:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>PageView:</strong> someone loaded a page</li>
<li><strong>ViewContent:</strong> someone looked at a product</li>
<li><strong>AddToCart:</strong> someone added a product to their cart</li>
<li><strong>InitiateCheckout:</strong> someone started checkout</li>
<li><strong>Purchase:</strong> someone bought, ideally reported with the order value</li>
<li><strong>Lead:</strong> someone submitted their details</li>
<li><strong>Contact:</strong> someone reached out, for example by tapping a WhatsApp button</li>
</ul>
<p>You can also create custom events for actions specific to your business, such as &quot;booked consultation.&quot;</p>
<h2>The Conversions API: the backup reporter</h2>
<p>Browsers, ad blockers and phone privacy settings stop the pixel from seeing everything. The <strong>Conversions API</strong> solves much of this by sending the same events directly from your server or platform to Meta.</p>
<p>Running both together is the standard setup today. Meta removes duplicates, so a purchase reported by both the pixel and the server counts once, as long as both send the same event ID.</p>
<p>All of these events land in a <strong>dataset</strong> inside Events Manager, which is Meta&#39;s name for the container that holds your event data from the website, the server, apps and messaging.</p>
<h2>Event Match Quality</h2>
<p>For each server event, Events Manager shows an <strong>Event Match Quality</strong> score. It reflects how well Meta can match your events to real people on its platforms, based on the customer information you send with them, like email, phone and city. This information is hashed (scrambled) before it is sent, so it is not readable in plain text.</p>
<p>Better matching means better reporting and better optimization. If your score is low, sending phone numbers and emails with your events is usually the first fix.</p>
<h2>A worked example: reading a simple account</h2>
<p><em>An example account with invented numbers, to show how the pieces fit together.</em></p>
<p>Imagine a small online bakery that delivers in Beirut.</p>

<li><strong>Ad set:</strong> Beirut and surroundings, ages 22 to 55, automatic placements, optimizing for Purchase</li>
<li><strong>Ads:</strong> three videos, one showing a cake being decorated, one with a customer unboxing, one with the owner talking about ingredients</li>
</ul>
