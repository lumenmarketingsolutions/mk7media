12:T1ad0,<p>To promote a new business online with a small budget, start with the free channels that bring customers who are already looking (Google Business Profile, a clear Instagram profile and WhatsApp Business), then post simple, useful content from your phone, collect reviews from your first customers, and only then put a small ad budget behind the posts that are already working. Spending on ads before these basics are in place is the most common way new businesses waste money.</p>
<p>A small budget forces you to be focused, which is often an advantage. You cannot be everywhere, so you pick the one or two places your customers spend time and do them well.</p>
<p>Here is the order we recommend, with a sample budget at the end.</p>
<h2>1. Set up the free profiles customers check first</h2>
<p>Before anyone buys from a new business, they look it up. Make sure what they find looks real and trustworthy.</p>
<p><strong>Google Business Profile.</strong> If you have a physical location or serve a local area, this is the free listing that appears on Google Maps and in local searches. Fill in everything: category, hours, phone, WhatsApp link, photos of the place and the team, and a short description in the words customers would search. Post updates there too.</p>
<p><strong>Instagram (and Facebook).</strong> Your profile is your shop window. Use a clear profile photo, a bio that says what you do and where (&quot;Handmade leather bags, delivered across Lebanon&quot;), a link to your WhatsApp or website, and highlights for prices, delivery, reviews and FAQs.</p>
<p><strong>WhatsApp Business.</strong> The free WhatsApp Business app lets you add a business profile, a catalog of products, a greeting message and quick replies for common questions. In Lebanon and the Gulf, many customers will message you before anything else, so this is often your real storefront.</p>
<h2>2. Make content that answers real questions</h2>
<p>You do not need a production team. Most small businesses grow with content shot on a phone. What matters is that it is useful or interesting to the person you want to sell to.</p>
<p>Good content ideas for a new business:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Show how it is made or done.</strong> Packing orders, preparing food, a stylist at work.</li>
<li><strong>Answer the questions people ask you on WhatsApp.</strong> Each common question is a post or a short video.</li>
<li><strong>Show the product in use.</strong> On a real person, in a real home, in a real setting your customer recognizes.</li>
<li><strong>Introduce yourself and your team.</strong> People buy from people, especially from new businesses.</li>
<li><strong>Share customer reactions,</strong> with their permission.</li>
</ul>
<p>Post consistently. Three good posts a week for three months will do more than a burst of daily posts for two weeks followed by silence.</p>
<h2>3. Get your first reviews early</h2>
<p>Reviews are the cheapest trust you can build. After every sale in your first months, ask the customer for a review on Google or a short testimonial you can share. Make it easy: send the link directly on WhatsApp right after delivery, when they are happiest.</p>
<p>Ten honest reviews on Google can change how a new business looks in local search results, and they make every ad you run later more convincing.</p>
<h2>4. Use your network and local partners</h2>
<p>Free and cheap promotion is often offline first:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ask friends and early customers to share your page, specifically, with one person who would care.</li>
<li>Partner with a business that serves the same customer, such as a bridal shop teaming up with a makeup artist, or a gym with a healthy meal service.</li>
<li>Send products to small local creators whose audience matches yours. Smaller accounts often have closer, more trusting followers than big ones.</li>
<li>Offer a referral reward, like a discount for both the customer and the friend they bring.</li>
</ul>
<h2>5. Put a small ad budget behind what already works</h2>
<p>Once you have a few weeks of posts, look at which ones got the most saves, shares, comments and messages. Those posts have already proven people care. Turn them into ads instead of creating new ads from scratch.</p>
<p>For most new local businesses, two simple campaigns are enough:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>A Meta campaign that sends people to WhatsApp</strong>, using your best two or three posts or videos as ads, targeted broadly to your city or country.</li>
<li><strong>A small Google Search campaign</strong> on your most specific keywords, but only if people actually search for what you offer. Check first with Google&#39;s free Keyword Planner.</li>
</ol>
<p>Install the Meta pixel on your website (if you have one) from day one, even before you run ads, so data starts collecting. If you sell through WhatsApp, keep a simple list of which customers came from ads so you know what is working.</p>
<h2>A worked example: a $300 first month</h2>
<p><em>A sample plan with example numbers, for a new home bakery in Beirut. Real results depend on your product, prices and market.</em></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>$0:</strong> Google Business Profile, Instagram profile, WhatsApp Business catalog and quick replies</li>
<li><strong>$0:</strong> 12 posts and short videos shot on a phone across the month</li>
<li><strong>$40:</strong> samples sent to three small local food creators</li>
<li><strong>$260:</strong> a Meta campaign to WhatsApp, about $9 per day, using the three best performing posts</li>
</ul>
<p>Suppose the ads bring 130 WhatsApp conversations at about $2 each, and 20 percent order. That is 26 orders. At $35 per average order, that is $910 in sales from $260 of ad spend, plus the reviews, followers and repeat customers that come with them.</p>
<p>If the numbers look worse, look first at the offer, the photos and how fast you reply on WhatsApp. Spending more rarely fixes a problem in one of those.</p>
<h2>6. Keep a simple weekly routine</h2>
<p>A small budget works best with steady habits. Once a week:</p>
<ul>
<li>Check which posts and ads brought messages or sales</li>
<li>Reply to every comment, review and message</li>
<li>Ask every new customer for a review</li>
<li>Plan next week&#39;s content around what worked</li>
</ul>
