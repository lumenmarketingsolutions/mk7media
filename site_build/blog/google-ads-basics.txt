12:T1e4f,<p>Google search ads beat social ads when people already know they need what you sell and are actively searching for it. Someone typing &quot;emergency AC repair Dubai&quot; or &quot;dentist near me Beirut&quot; has a problem right now, and showing up at the top of that search is often the fastest way to get a customer. Social ads on Instagram and Facebook are better at creating demand: showing people something they did not know they wanted.</p>
<p>So the simple rule is this. If people search for your product or service, use Google search ads to catch them. If they do not search for it yet, use Meta ads to make them want it. Most growing businesses end up using both.</p>
<p>Here is how Google ads work and how to tell which one your business should start with.</p>
<h2>How Google search ads work</h2>
<p>You choose <strong>keywords</strong>, which are the words and phrases you want your ads to show for. When someone searches something related, Google runs a quick auction among advertisers. The winner is not only the highest bidder. Google also looks at how relevant your ad and landing page are to the search, which it summarizes as <strong>Quality Score</strong>.</p>
<p>You usually pay only when someone clicks. That price is your <strong>cost per click</strong> (CPC). It varies a lot by industry and country. Legal, medical and real estate keywords tend to be expensive. Niche local services can be cheap.</p>
<h2>Keyword match types in plain words</h2>
<p>Match types control how closely a search must match your keyword:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Exact match</strong> shows your ad for searches with the same meaning as your keyword. It gives you the most control.</li>
<li><strong>Phrase match</strong> shows your ad when the search includes the meaning of your phrase, with other words around it.</li>
<li><strong>Broad match</strong> shows your ad for anything Google considers related. It gives you the most reach and the least control, and it works best with good conversion tracking and smart bidding.</li>
</ul>
<p>You can also add <strong>negative keywords</strong>, which are words you do not want to show up for. A cosmetic clinic might add &quot;free,&quot; &quot;course&quot; and &quot;jobs&quot; so it does not pay for people looking for training or work.</p>
<h2>Search campaigns vs Performance Max</h2>
<p>Google offers several campaign types. The two you will hear most about are:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Search campaigns:</strong> text ads on Google search results, controlled by your keywords. They are clear, easy to understand and a good starting point for most service businesses.</li>
<li><strong>Performance Max (PMax):</strong> one campaign that runs across Google search, YouTube, Display, Gmail, Maps and Discover, with Google&#39;s AI deciding where to show your ads. It can work very well for ecommerce stores with a product feed and solid conversion data, but it gives you less visibility into exactly where the money goes.</li>
</ul>
<p>Our usual advice for a new advertiser: start with a focused Search campaign on your highest intent keywords. Add Performance Max once tracking is solid and you have steady conversions.</p>
<h2>When search ads beat social ads</h2>
<p>Google search usually wins when:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>There is clear search demand.</strong> People type your service into Google. Think plumbers, clinics, lawyers, car rentals, moving companies, B2B software.</li>
<li><strong>The need is urgent,</strong> like a broken AC in August in the Gulf, a burst pipe or a last minute gift.</li>
<li><strong>The customer compares before buying.</strong> People search &quot;best&quot;, &quot;price&quot;, &quot;near me&quot; and reviews.</li>
<li><strong>Your product is hard to show visually.</strong> Accounting software or insurance does not make a scroll stopping Reel.</li>
</ul>
<p>Meta ads usually win when:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The product is visual or impulse driven.</strong> Fashion, beauty, food, home decor.</li>
<li><strong>People do not search for it yet.</strong> A new product category or a new brand.</li>
<li><strong>You want to build demand</strong> that later shows up as searches for your brand name.</li>
</ul>
<h2>How to check search demand in your market</h2>
<p>Before you spend, check whether people actually search for what you sell in your country:</p>
<ol>
<li>Open <strong>Google Keyword Planner</strong> inside Google Ads (it is free with an account).</li>
<li>Enter a few phrases your customers would type, like &quot;laser hair removal Beirut&quot; or &quot;office cleaning Doha.&quot;</li>
<li>Set the location to your target country or city.</li>
<li>Look at the estimated monthly searches and the suggested bid ranges.</li>
</ol>
<p>In smaller markets like Lebanon, search volumes for some terms can be low, and many searches are in a mix of English, Arabic and French, or Arabic written in Latin letters. Check several versions of each phrase.</p>
<h2>Set up conversion tracking before you launch</h2>
<p>Google, like Meta, needs to know which clicks turned into customers. Set up conversions for the actions that matter:</p>
<ul>
<li>Form submissions and booked appointments</li>
<li>Calls from the ad or from your website</li>
<li>WhatsApp button clicks</li>
<li>Purchases, with the order value, for online stores</li>
</ul>
<p>Use the Google tag on your site, or connect through Google Tag Manager or your platform&#39;s native integration. <strong>Enhanced conversions</strong>, which send hashed customer details like email with each conversion, help Google match more of your results.</p>
<p>Once Google has conversion data, you can use smart bidding strategies like <strong>Maximize conversions</strong> or <strong>Target CPA</strong> (a target cost per acquisition) and let Google set bids for each auction.</p>
<h2>A worked example</h2>
<p><em>Illustrative numbers only, to show how to judge whether search ads make sense.</em></p>
<p>An AC maintenance company in Dubai checks Keyword Planner and sees decent search volume for &quot;AC repair Dubai&quot; and similar terms, with an estimated cost of around $2 per click.</p>
<p>They run a Search campaign at $40 per day for 30 days: $1,200 total, about 600 clicks.</p>
<ul>
<li>Their landing page converts 8 percent of visitors into calls or WhatsApp messages: 48 inquiries.</li>
<li>Their team books 50 percent of inquiries: 24 jobs.</li>
<li>Each job is worth $150 on average: $3,600 revenue.</li>
</ul>
<p>They spent $1,200 to make $3,600. Whether that is profitable depends on their margins, but it gives them a clear number to improve. A better landing page that converts at 12 percent instead of 8 would bring 72 inquiries from the same spend.</p>
<p>The same company might find Meta ads bring cheaper inquiries, but from people who were not urgently looking for a repair. Testing both with proper tracking is the only honest way to know.</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Sending ads to the homepage.</strong> Send each group of keywords to a page that matches the search.</li>
<li><strong>No negative keywords.</strong> You pay for irrelevant searches.</li>
<li><strong>Broad match without conversion tracking.</strong> Google has no signal to guide it.</li>
<li><strong>Judging after three days.</strong> Give campaigns a few weeks and enough clicks to see a pattern.</li>
</ul>
