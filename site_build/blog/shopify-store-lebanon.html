Launching a Shopify store in Lebanon: what to get right first

November 13, 2026 · 6 min read · MK7 Media

When you launch a Shopify store in Lebanon, the things to get right first are how customers pay (cash on delivery and whichever card options are available to you), how delivery works and what it costs, a product page that looks great on a phone, a fast way to reach you on WhatsApp, and tracking that tells Meta and Google who bought. The theme and the logo matter much less than these.

Shopify itself works well for Lebanese businesses. It is reliable, easy for your team to manage, and connects with the ad platforms you will use to grow. The local work is in the details around it: payment, delivery, language and trust.

Here is the order we would tackle it in.

1. Sort out payments before anything else

Payments decide how many people finish checkout, so start here.

Cash on delivery. For many Lebanese shoppers, paying cash at the door is the default, and it builds trust with a store they have never bought from. Shopify lets you add cash on delivery as a manual payment method, so you can offer it from day one.

Card payments. Which card payment providers you can use depends on where your business and bank account are registered. Check Shopify's list of supported payment providers for your country, and talk to your bank or a local payment provider about what integrates with Shopify. If you also sell to the Gulf or abroad, your options for those customers may be different.

Currency. Many Lebanese stores price in USD, since that is how customers already think about prices. Whatever you choose, be consistent across the store, your ads and your WhatsApp replies.

2. Make delivery clear and predictable

Customers want to know three things before they order: where you deliver, how much it costs and when it will arrive.

  • Set up shipping zones and rates in Shopify that match your actual delivery partner's pricing.
  • Show delivery cost and timing on the product page, not only at checkout. A surprise fee at the last step is one of the most common reasons people abandon a cart.
  • If you offer free delivery above a certain order value, say so near the price.
  • Addresses in Lebanon are often described by area, street and landmarks. Test your checkout form so it is easy for customers to give directions your driver can actually use, and require a phone number.

3. Confirm cash on delivery orders on WhatsApp

Cash on delivery comes with a cost: some customers refuse the package or are not home. A simple confirmation message cuts a lot of that waste.

When an order comes in, send the customer a WhatsApp message with the order summary and ask them to confirm. Only ship confirmed orders, or call the ones who do not reply. This can be done manually at first and automated later through WhatsApp automation.

4. Design for the phone first

Almost all of your traffic from Instagram and Facebook will be on a phone. Open your store on your own phone and go through it as a customer would.

Check that:

  • Product photos load fast and show the product clearly
  • The price and "Add to cart" button are visible without scrolling much
  • Size guides, materials and delivery info are easy to find
  • Checkout takes as few steps as possible
  • A WhatsApp button is visible but does not cover the "Buy" button

Pick a clean, fast theme over a flashy one. Fewer apps usually means a faster store, so only install what you truly need.

5. Write product pages that answer questions

Every question a customer has to ask on WhatsApp is a chance for them to leave. Good product pages answer the common ones upfront:

  • What exactly is it, and who is it for?
  • What size, color or variant should I choose?
  • How long does delivery take, and what does it cost?
  • Can I return or exchange it?
  • Is it real? Reviews, customer photos and clear brand information help a lot here.

Decide which language your customers want to read. Many Lebanese stores work well in English, some benefit from Arabic or French, and stores targeting the Gulf often need Arabic. Shopify supports multiple languages, so you can add them as you grow.

6. Install tracking properly

Before you run a single ad, connect your store to Meta and Google:

  • Meta: install the Facebook and Instagram sales channel app and connect your pixel and dataset. This sets up the pixel and the Conversions API (server side tracking) so purchase events reach Meta reliably.
  • Google: connect Google Analytics and Google Ads through Shopify's Google channel or the Google tag, and make sure purchases are tracked as conversions.

One important detail for cash on delivery: Shopify will report a purchase when the order is placed, even if the customer later refuses it. Keep an eye on your real delivered and paid orders so your ad numbers are not too optimistic.

A worked example

Example numbers to show how the details add up. These are not from a real store.

Say your store gets 2,000 visitors a month from ads, and 2 percent place an order. That is 40 orders.

If 25 percent of cash on delivery orders are refused, you deliver 30. At a $40 average order, that is $1,200 in real revenue.

Now you add a WhatsApp confirmation step and cut refusals to 10 percent. You deliver 36 orders, $1,440.

Then you show delivery cost on the product page and simplify checkout, and the order rate rises from 2 percent to 2.5 percent. That is 50 orders, 45 delivered, $1,800.

With the same ads and the same traffic, revenue grew by 50 percent, just from getting the basics right.

7. Launch small, then scale

You do not need 300 products on day one. Start with your best sellers, make those pages excellent, and run ads to them. Watch where people drop off: product page, cart or checkout. Fix the biggest leak first, then add products and budget.
